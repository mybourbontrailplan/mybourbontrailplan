What Is Kentucky BourbonFest?

The short version: it's the biggest bourbon festival in the world, held every September in Bardstown, KY. The 2026 edition runs September 10-13, and it has sold out again. Here's what's still available, and what to know before you go.

10 min read
Updated September 2026
Ticket status checked against the official site

The Short Answer

Kentucky BourbonFest, officially the Kentucky Bourbon Festival, is an annual multi-day celebration held in Bardstown, Kentucky, the self-proclaimed Bourbon Capital of the World. It takes place every September during National Bourbon Heritage Month, and in 2026 it marks its 35th year.

The numbers tell the story: 60+ distilleries pouring 200+ bourbons, master distillers you can actually walk up and talk to, a bourbon marketplace, exclusive bottle purchases you can't get anywhere else, and attendees from over a dozen countries. It started in 1992 as a dinner for 250 people. It now sells out every year.

2026 Status: Festival Week

September 10-13, 2026, in Bardstown, Kentucky. Tickets went on sale April 14 and the festival has sold out, as it has every year since it went ticketed in 2021. Weekend Taster passes, all four VIP tiers and the Thursday Bourbon in the Air event are gone. As of September 5 the $115 Sunday Sampler was still listed as available, and it is the only remaining way in. Full details and the current status are on the official ticket page.

What Actually Happens at BourbonFest

The core of the festival is unlimited bourbon sampling. Your ticket gets you access to the tasting floor where distillery reps, often the master distillers themselves, pour expressions you'd struggle to find anywhere else. It's not a crowded bar situation where you queue for a half-ounce pour of something mass-market. Distilleries bring their best stuff, including allocated bottles and festival-exclusive single barrels.

Beyond the tasting floor:

  • Bourbon:30 Talk Show: Thirteen 30-minute panels with big names from the bourbon industry, plus a couple of extended hour-long sessions. Think less formal lecture, more after-hours conversation with people who actually make the whiskey. Sessions run 12:30 to 5:30 PM Friday and Saturday and 12:30 to 3:00 PM Sunday, and they're included with any pass.
  • Master Distillers' Bottle Auction: Bottles signed by master distillers, rare releases, and one-of-a-kind items, run by the Oscar Getz Museum in the chapel at Spalding Hall. It's open to the public and does not require a festival ticket, which makes it the one piece of the weekend you can still walk into if you never got a pass.
  • Bourbon Marketplace: Bourbon-themed goods, glassware, accessories, and food, feeding into the Bourbon Capital Bites area. Good place to pick up gifts if you're the kind of person who comes home from every trip with a bag full of stuff for other people.
  • Bourbon Capital Bites: Food options curated to pair with bourbon. Bardstown has genuinely good food and the festival brings some of that into the event footprint. Eat before 2 PM on Sunday, some of the trucks shut down then.
  • Exclusive bottle purchases: Distillery booths often sell bottles directly, including festival-exclusive single barrel selections you can only get there. If you're a collector, this alone can justify the ticket price.

Weekend Taster holders get unlimited re-entry across all three days, so you're not locked into a single session. That matters more than it sounds: general admission doors open at noon and the tasting floor closes at 6 PM Friday and Saturday, so each day is a six-hour window rather than an all-day marathon. Most people spread it across two days. Saturday tends to be the biggest, with Friday and Sunday somewhat more relaxed.

Heads Up

Individual premium events (specialty tastings, dinners, masterclasses) are often ticketed separately on top of your base pass. Budget for these if you're interested, they tend to be the most memorable parts of the weekend and sell out fast.

Where It's Held, Hours, and What to Bring

The festival grounds are at Spalding Hall, 114 N. Fifth Street, Bardstown, KY 40004, in the middle of downtown, about 40 miles south of Louisville. Bardstown is a small town, the kind where you can walk between the festival grounds, restaurants, and your hotel without needing a car. That's intentional. You're not going to want to drive after a full day on the tasting floor.

Daily Hours

  • Thursday, Sept. 10: 6:00 to 9:30 PM, the Bourbon in the Air kickoff only. The main tasting floor is not open to general admission on Thursday.
  • Friday and Saturday, Sept. 11-12: noon to 6 PM. VIP tiers get in at 11 AM.
  • Sunday, Sept. 13: noon to 4 PM. This is the Sunday Sampler window.

Before You Leave the House

  • Your wristband comes in the mail. Admission is by RFID wristband mailed out ahead of the festival, not a ticket you print or pull up on your phone. If you bought a pass and nothing has arrived, sort that out with the festival now, not in the entry line.
  • Clear bag policy. Clear bags up to 12" x 6" x 12", small clutches up to 4.5" x 6.5", or an empty tote. Every bag is subject to search, and refusing the search means you don't get in.
  • Leave at home: outside alcohol, weapons, large bags, pets, and tents.
  • Bring cash for parking. There is no festival lot. It's street parking and nearby private lots within walking distance, and those lots are cash.

Getting There Without Driving

Most people drive from Louisville or Lexington and base themselves in Bardstown for the weekend. The drive from Louisville is about 45 minutes, easy and scenic through rolling Nelson County farmland. If you're staying in Louisville and would rather not drive at all, the festival runs a shuttle from the Tempo by Hilton Louisville Downtown NuLu for $30 a day, roughly an hour each way with the schedule varying by day. Given that the entire point of the afternoon is unlimited pours, a shuttle seat or a booked rideshare is worth more than the time it costs you.

Lodging, If You're Still Looking

Bardstown has limited hotel inventory and it is effectively gone for festival weekend by now. Louisville is the realistic fallback, 45 minutes away with far more rooms and the shuttle option above. A vacation rental in the countryside around Bardstown is the other route, and often the last thing still bookable. For 2027, treat lodging as urgent the moment tickets drop in April: downtown properties fill months out, well before most people who bought passes get around to booking a room. Is It Worth It?

At $225 the Weekend Taster buys three days of pours from 60+ distilleries, face time with master distillers, exclusive bottle opportunities, and the full atmosphere of the world's premier bourbon event. That is a genuinely good deal, which is why it sells out. At $115 for four hours, the Sunday Sampler is a slightly worse rate per hour but a much easier yes: it gets you the same tasting floor, the same Bourbon:30 sessions and the same bottle access, and it is the one that's still on sale. If you're a bourbon drinker who's ever thought about visiting Kentucky, combining BourbonFest with a broader Bourbon Trail trip in September is one of the best ways to structure that trip.

The festival lands squarely in what we call peak bourbon season, September weather in Kentucky is ideal, warehouse temperatures are dropping back into comfortable range for tours, and fall foliage is starting on the rural drives between distilleries. It's the best time of year to be on the trail regardless of the festival.

The catch is the crowds. Festival weekend is the busiest weekend of the year in Bardstown. If you're pairing BourbonFest with distillery tours, schedule those tours before or after the festival dates, not during. Maker's Mark and Buffalo Trace in particular are harder to get into during festival week.

How to Pair BourbonFest with a Full Bourbon Trail Trip

The tasting floor runs Friday through Sunday, with Thursday evening's Bourbon in the Air as a separate ticketed kickoff. A good structure for a first-time Bourbon Trail visitor combining both:

  • Monday–Wednesday: Louisville distilleries, Old Forester, Angel's Envy, Evan Williams, and Whiskey Row tasting rooms. Book these well in advance; they fill up during festival week.
  • Wednesday afternoon: Drive to Bardstown. Get settled, grab dinner, orient yourself before the crowds arrive.
  • Thursday evening: Bourbon in the Air, if you bought the add-on. Otherwise an early dinner and a quiet night, because Friday starts at noon and runs on bourbon.
  • Friday to Sunday: BourbonFest. Use the re-entry to pace yourself: six hours Friday, six Saturday, four on Sunday. Don't skip the Bourbon:30 sessions.
  • Monday: Recover in Bardstown, then hit Maker's Mark in Loretto (book ahead) on the way out, it's 30 minutes from downtown Bardstown and worth the stop.

That's a 7–8 day trip that covers Louisville, the festival, and the Bardstown/Loretto region without feeling rushed. It's the same window our itinerary guide covers if you want a day-by-day breakdown.
