Monk's Road Boiler House: Log Still on Whiskey Row

The upscale option on Whiskey Row, and the first distillery tasting room on the strip to offer full dining. This is Log Still Distillery's Louisville outpost: the Monk's Road whiskey is distilled down in New Haven, about an hour south.

What to Expect

Monk's Road Boiler House opened in July 2024 in a beautifully restored industrial space at 131 W Main, and it does something none of the other Whiskey Row rooms do: it is a serious restaurant as well as a tasting room. It pairs Monk's Road pours with an upscale chophouse menu, and it was the first distillery tasting room on the strip to offer full dining.

Nothing is distilled here. The stills and rickhouses are at Log Still's campus in New Haven, about an hour south, so this is a place to eat and pour rather than a production stop.

Its position matters to how you use it. It sits at the east end of the Whiskey Row walk, so it can bookend your day from either side: lunch before you work west, or a proper sit-down dinner after a day of tasting. Check the clock before you count on lunch, though. It opens at noon Thursday through Saturday, but not until 3 PM Monday through Wednesday and 5 PM on Sunday, so the lunch version of this plan only exists on a Thursday, Friday, or Saturday. Every other day it is a dinner stop. If you want a real meal in the Whiskey Row corridor rather than bar snacks between pours, this is the one. Prices match the setting, so plan accordingly.

How to Visit

Treat this as a restaurant rather than a drop-in tasting room: if you want a table, book it. It sits at the east end of the strip near Angel's Envy, which makes it the natural first or last stop on a Whiskey Row walk rather than something you squeeze in mid-block. It is open seven days, but the opening time moves: noon Thursday through Saturday, 3 PM Monday through Wednesday, and 5 PM Sunday. Friday and Saturday it runs to 11:45 PM, which makes it the latest kitchen on the Row by a wide margin.

The Bottom Line

This is the upscale option on Whiskey Row, and it solves a problem the rest of the strip ignores: where to get a real meal without breaking up your day. The restored boiler house is the best-looking room on the Row, and it is the only distillery tasting room here that will sit you down to a proper chophouse dinner rather than bar snacks between pours. Prices match the setting, so it is not the cheapest stop you will make, but you are paying for a restaurant, not a tasting fee. Time it deliberately: Thursday through Saturday it opens at noon and you can put it early in the walk as lunch, but the rest of the week it opens mid-afternoon or later, which makes it dinner at the end instead. If you want to see where Monk's Road is actually made, that is a separate trip to Log Still in New Haven, about an hour south.
