What's on the Printed Map

This isn't a screenshot of a phone map dumped onto a page. It's laid out to be read on paper and used in the car. Here's what you get across the two pages.

Page One: The Statewide Map

  • Numbered pins for 60+ distilleries, placed on a clean outline of Kentucky so you can see how the trail actually spreads across the state.
  • Region color-coding, so Louisville, Bardstown, Frankfort, Lexington/Lawrenceburg, Northern Kentucky, and Western Kentucky each read as their own cluster at a glance.

Why a Paper Map Still Earns Its Spot

It's fair to ask why anyone prints a map anymore. On the bourbon trail, there are two honest reasons, and neither is nostalgia.

Cell service disappears exactly where you need it. The heart of bourbon country runs through rural Nelson, Washington, and Marion counties, the two-lane roads that connect the Bardstown-area distilleries to Loretto and beyond. Coverage out there is patchy at best. The moment you're between Maker's Mark and Bardstown with one bar and a spinning map, a printed sheet that shows every distillery and its number is worth more than the nicest navigation app.

A paper map doesn't die at 4 PM on a tour day. A full day of photos, mobile boarding passes for tastings, and rideshare apps drains a phone fast. Your map shouldn't go dark just because your battery did. Paper doesn't need charging, signal, or a data plan, and it's readable in bright sun on a distillery lawn where your screen washes out.

Don't rely on signal in the Bardstown countryside

The single most common "my plan fell apart" story we hear is losing navigation on the rural stretch between the Bardstown cluster and Maker's Mark in Loretto. Print the map before you leave your hotel's wifi, and you'll never be stuck squinting at a frozen screen on a country road.

How to Print It So It Comes Out Right

The PDF is built to print cleanly on an ordinary home or office printer. A few pointers so the first copy is the keeper:

  • Paper size: designed for standard US Letter (8.5 x 11 in), landscape. It also scales fine to A4 if you set your printer to "fit to page."
  • Two pages: page one is the map, page two is the reference list and checklist. Print both, or just the page you want, they stand on their own.
  • Black and white is fine: pins are numbered and the list is plain text, so nothing depends on color. Grayscale prints stay fully legible; color just makes the regions pop.
  • Fold it in thirds and it tucks neatly into a glovebox, a jacket pocket, or the seat-back pocket of a tour van.

Printable Bourbon Trail Map FAQ

Is the printable Kentucky Bourbon Trail map free?

Yes. It's a free two-page PDF. The only thing we ask for is an email address so we can send it to you, and you can unsubscribe anytime. There's no charge and no catch.

What's actually on the printable bourbon trail map?

Page one is a statewide map with 60+ distilleries shown as numbered pins, color-coded by region, plus a zoomed inset of the busy Central corridor and a region legend. Page two is a reference list grouped by region: each distillery's number, city, whether it's on the official trail or a craft producer, booking difficulty, and tour cost, alongside a drive-times table and a visited checklist so you can tick off stops as you go.

Can I print the bourbon trail map in black and white?

Yes. The map is designed to stay legible in grayscale. Pins are numbered rather than relying on color alone, and the reference list and checklist are plain text, so a black-and-white home or office printer works fine. Color makes the regions pop, but it isn't required to use the map.

Do I need cell service to use the printed map?

No, and that's the point. Signal drops out on the rural roads between distilleries in Nelson, Washington, and Marion counties, exactly where you're navigating between Bardstown-area stops. A printed map works with no bars, no battery, and no data plan.

How is the printable map different from the interactive map?

The interactive map is for planning at home: search a distillery, filter by region, tap a pin for tour details, and build a route. The printable PDF is for the road: a fixed, offline reference you can fold into a glovebox and mark up by hand. Most people use the interactive map to plan, then print the PDF to carry.
