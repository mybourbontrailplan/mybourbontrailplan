Louisville Bourbon Trail Map

Louisville is the only place on the trail where a full bourbon day needs no car at all, provided you pick the right blocks. West Main Street is the reason: a run of distilleries close enough that you can walk from one end to the other in about fifteen minutes.

How These Distilleries Cluster

Louisville looks like one dense blob on the map, and at overview zoom that is misleading. It is really four groups with very different transport needs, and the difference between a relaxed day and a frustrating one is knowing which group you booked into.

West Main Street, the Whiskey Row core

All within a 15-minute walk

Old Forester at 119 W Main, Evan Williams at 528, Buzzard's Roost at 624 and Michter's Fort Nelson at 801, with Kentucky Peerless one block north at 120 N 10th. The street numbers are the whole trick: roughly two minutes of walking per block, so end to end is about fifteen minutes. Nothing here needs a car.

NuLu and Butchertown, east of 1st Street

20 to 30 minutes on foot from the Row

Angel's Envy at 500 E Main, Whiskey Thief's tasting room, Rabbit Hole at 711 E Jefferson and Copper & Kings out at 1121 E Washington. Walkable from each other, but a long walk from the west end of the Row. Treat this as its own half of the day, or budget a short rideshare between the two halves.

Shelbyville, straight out I-64

About 30 minutes east, 5 minutes apart

Bulleit and Jeptha Creed sit 2.3 miles apart, which makes them a natural pair and a poor single destination. If you are driving out, do both. Big free lots at each, and Bulleit's campus is large enough that parking is never a thought.

The ones on their own

Stitzel-Weller 10 min southwest, Kentucky Artisan 20 min northeast

Stitzel-Weller in Shively is the closest of the outliers and the one bourbon people care most about, being the historic Pappy Van Winkle site. Kentucky Artisan is up in Crestwood, twenty minutes the other way, which makes pairing the two a bad idea: they are forty minutes apart through the city.

A Sensible One-Day Loop

The car-free version, and the one we recommend. Start at the west end of West Main with a mid-morning tour, walk east through the Row hitting one or two more, and eat lunch on Main without moving your car. Rideshare to NuLu in the early afternoon for Angel's Envy and finish on their rooftop. Three tours, one short ride, no parking decisions after the first one.

The driving version. Two on the Row in the morning, then out I-64 to the Bulleit and Jeptha Creed pair in the afternoon. This one needs a designated driver, and it is the version where three stops is genuinely the ceiling.

What does not work is mixing NuLu and Shelbyville in the same day. They are opposite directions from downtown, and you will spend the afternoon in a car looking at the interstate.

Where the Lunch Break Falls

You do not need to plan hard here, which is unusual on this trail. West Main has food on the same blocks as the distilleries, so lunch is a matter of walking out of one tour and into a room. Feast BBQ is right on Whiskey Row and fast enough to keep a tour schedule; Doc Crow's is the sit-down option on the same stretch.

The thing worth actually planning is the gap. Tours run 60 to 90 minutes and most reservations are on the hour, so a 1 PM lunch tends to collide with everyone else's. Eat at noon or at 2 and the Row is noticeably calmer. Our Louisville eat and drink section covers dinner and the bourbon bars, which are the actual reason to stay downtown for the evening.

Parking Realities

No distillery on Whiskey Row has its own lot. Not one. It is street parking and public garages for all of them, which sounds worse than it is: park once in a garage near 1st and Main, then do not touch the car again until you leave. The mistake is treating each distillery as a separate drive and paying for parking four times.

Budget for it. Downtown Louisville hotels charge roughly $20 to $30 a night for parking on top of the room, per our budget guide, and daytime garages are extra.

East of 1st Street it changes. Rabbit Hole relies on NuLu street parking, which gets tight on weekends, while Copper & Kings has a free lot on site. Every one of the outliers, Stitzel-Weller, Kentucky Artisan, Jeptha Creed and Bulleit, has free on-site parking. The pattern is simple: downtown you pay and walk, outside downtown you park at the door.

Plan the Rest of the Day

Several more tasting rooms sit on these blocks without a pin on the map. Monk's Road Boiler House at 131 W Main, Green River at 714 and Bardstown Bourbon Co. at 730 are all real places you can walk into, but they land within a few pixels of the distilleries already pinned, so mapping them would produce one unreadable blob. They are walk-up sub-stops of blocks you are already standing on. Our Whiskey Row walking guide puts the whole corridor in order, including those.

For a day beyond Louisville, Bardstown is about 50 minutes south and Frankfort is about an hour east. Neither is a day trip you want to bolt onto a full Louisville day.
