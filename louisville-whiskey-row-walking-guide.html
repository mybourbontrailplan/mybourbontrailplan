Louisville Whiskey Row Walking Guide

Distillery tours, speakeasies, the best Cuban food in Kentucky, bourbon shopping, and the Louisville Slugger Museum, all without moving your car. Here's how to spend a full day on foot.

Louisville's Whiskey Row on West Main Street is the most walkable bourbon experience in Kentucky. The Main Street corridor alone packs in a dozen-odd distilleries and tasting rooms, and you can work through them, eat outstanding food, shop for bottles you actually can't find at home, and squeeze in the Louisville Slugger Museum for the non-bourbon-drinkers in your group, all in a single day on foot. No car, no designated driver logistics, no driving between rural distilleries.

This is also one of the most exciting stretches of bourbon real estate in the country right now. Several new tasting rooms and speakeasies have opened since 2024, and the NuLu neighborhood just east of Main Street keeps adding destinations worth the 10-minute walk. The corridor from the west end of Whiskey Row to the east end of NuLu is roughly 1.5 miles and contains more bourbon per square foot than anywhere outside of a warehouse.

The Recommended Route

Walk it east to west. Main Street's numbers climb as you head west, so this order is a straight line rather than a zigzag: you start at Angel's Envy on East Main and finish at Kentucky Peerless on 10th. The route works fine in reverse if your tour times demand it, but book Evan Williams and Old Forester for the morning either way.

Start east: Angel's Envy (500 E Main), book ahead, one of the best tours in the city and a strong opener while your palate is fresh
Morning tours: Old Forester (119 W Main) then Evan Williams (528 W Main), book both in advance, they fill up
Lunch: Monk's Road Boiler House (131 W Main) for a proper sit-down meal, or Doc Crow's for BBQ
Afternoon walk-ins: Buzzard's Roost (624 W Main) → Green River (714 W Main) → Pursuit Spirits (722 W Main) → Bardstown Bourbon Co. (730 W Main)
Break: Louisville Slugger Museum (800 W Main), perfect palate reset, with Big Bat Bourbon's blending class in the same building
Finish west: Michter's Fort Nelson bar (801 W Main), then Kentucky Peerless (120 N 10th St), one block north
Back east to NuLu: rideshare or a 15-minute walk from the west end, Chicken Cock, Whiskey Thief, and Rabbit Hole are all here
Bottle shopping: Evergreen Liquors (720 E Market St, NuLu), do this before dinner
Dinner: La Bodeguita de Mima (725 E Market St), make a reservation

The Whiskey Row Distilleries & Tasting Rooms

Walk these east to west along Main Street. The street numbers climb as you head west, so the order below is a straight line rather than a zigzag, and once you're on West Main everything sits within about six blocks.

One distinction worth understanding before you go: some of these stops actually distill on Main Street and can show you production, while others are brand tasting rooms whose whiskey is made elsewhere in Kentucky and trucked in. Neither is a lesser visit, they're just different. A working distillery gets you the mash, the still, and the smell of fermentation. A tasting room usually gets you a better bar, a deeper pour list, and staff with time to talk. We've badged every stop below so you can build a day that mixes both instead of accidentally booking five of the same thing.

1

Angel's Envy

Working Distillery Book Ahead
500 E Main St Tours and tastings

The east end of the corridor and the right place to start. Angel's Envy distills on-site in a restored 19th-century building, and the tour is one of the best in the city, partly because the port-cask finishing gives them something genuinely distinctive to talk about rather than the same grain-to-barrel story you'll hear four more times today.

Book ahead. It's a popular tour and walk-up availability is unreliable, especially on weekends. Doing it first means you hit the most technical tour of the day while your palate and your attention are both still fresh.

2

Old Forester Distilling Co.

Working Distillery Book Ahead
119 W Main St Tours and tastings

America's first bottled bourbon, and they lean into the history hard, the original 1882 storefront is recreated right inside. This is a fully working distillery, so tour visits feel legitimate rather than theatrical. You can watch the production process through glass walls, which is more than most Whiskey Row stops offer, and they run their own cooperage on-site.

The gift shop has Old Forester Birthday Bourbon and other limited releases if you're lucky on timing. Book tours in advance. The tasting room is walk-in friendly for pours without a tour.

3

Monk's Road Boiler House

Tasting Room Opened July 2024
131 W Main St Fine dining + tasting room

Log Still Distillery's Louisville outpost is the upscale option on Whiskey Row. Monk's Road Boiler House opened in July 2024 in a beautifully restored industrial space and operates as both a serious restaurant and a tasting room for Log Still's lineup. The whiskey itself is distilled down at Log Still in New Haven, about an hour south, so this is a place to eat and pour rather than a production stop.

If you want a proper sit-down meal in the Whiskey Row corridor rather than bar snacks between pours, this is it, and its position early in the walk makes it a natural lunch. Prices match the setting, plan accordingly.

4

Evan Williams Bourbon Experience

Working Distillery Book Ahead
528 W Main St ~$18–25 per person

Heaven Hill's museum-style attraction on Whiskey Row. The Traditional Tour walks you through a recreation of Evan Williams' original 1783 distillery and into the working artisanal still on-site, it's genuinely educational and not just marketing theater. The Speakeasy Tour ($25) takes you into a period-accurate 1920s speakeasy tasting room for cocktails and small-batch pours.

Book online before your trip, this one fills up, especially on weekends. Pair it with Old Forester as your second morning tour, before lunch and before your palate has been worked over by the afternoon.

Full Evan Williams profile →
5

Buzzard's Roost

Working Distillery Walk-In Friendly
624 W Main St Opened 2023

One of the newer additions to Whiskey Row, and a good one. Buzzard's Roost has a cool, approachable vibe, a speakeasy lounge downstairs, a 75-gallon pot still on-site, and staff who actually know their stuff. They're known for double-oaked secondary maturation, built on 17 proprietary barrel types with specific char and toast levels, which is the most interesting production story on this block.

The atmosphere and the people behind the bar are what earn this stop on the route. Order the old fashioned flight, it's the thing to get. Walk-ins welcome, no reservation needed.

Full Buzzard's Roost profile →
6

Green River Distilling Co.

Tasting Room Opened 2025
714 W Main St Walk-in friendly

One of the most interesting new openings on the strip. Green River built a genuinely fun tasting room here: horseshoe-shaped bar, retail section with bottle engraving, and a hidden speakeasy entrance that's worth finding. The "Fill-Your-Spirits" experience lets you fill a jug directly from a barrel at barrel strength, the kind of thing you can't do at a standard distillery tour. The whiskey is distilled at Green River's home plant in Owensboro, out in Western Kentucky.

Green River's whiskey has been well-regarded for years and the Louisville tasting room finally gives it a flagship presence it deserves. Stop in for a pour and at minimum get your name on a bottle.

7

Pursuit Spirits

Tasting Room Opened 2025 Book Ahead
722 W Main St Tasting rooms + cocktail lounge

The team behind the Bourbon Pursuit podcast built a 7,600-square-foot, two-story home on Whiskey Row, and it's an unusual one. Pursuit doesn't distill: they're blenders, sourcing from award-winning distilleries and building expressions from the barrels they select. Their production facility is over on Mellwood Avenue and isn't open to visitors, so what you get on Main Street is the front of house, and they've made it the point rather than an apology.

Upstairs is a set of tasting rooms plus the podcast studio; downstairs is Trial + Error, a cocktail lounge with a deep pour list of Pursuit expressions. The experiences lean hands-on, barrel picks and bottling straight from the source. Booking ahead is recommended. Slot it in with Green River and Bardstown Bourbon Co. and you have three tasting rooms in a hundred feet, which is the densest stretch of the whole walk.

8

Bardstown Bourbon Company Tasting Room

Tasting Room Walk-In Friendly
730 W Main St Interactive tastings, exclusive releases

Bardstown Bourbon Company's Louisville outpost gives you access to the collaborative whiskeys and exclusive releases they're known for, without the drive down to Bardstown, where the whiskey is actually made. The tasting room has a modern, approachable feel and the staff does a good job guiding you through what makes BBC's sourcing-heavy model unusual in the industry. Worth stopping in if you haven't been to the main facility, or if you want to try releases only available here.

Full Bardstown Bourbon Co. profile →
9

Big Bat Bourbon

Tasting Room Book Ahead
800 W Main St (Louisville Slugger Museum) ~45 min blending class

Tucked into the Louisville Slugger Museum building, Big Bat is the one stop on the Row where you don't taste someone else's blend, you make your own. The 45-minute class walks you through blending from six sourced bourbons, then you bottle and label what you built and take it home. Nothing is distilled here; the whiskey comes in from elsewhere and the craft on offer is the blending itself.

It pairs naturally with the Slugger factory tour in the same building, which makes this block the obvious mid-route break: one person does the bat factory, another builds a bourbon, and everybody's palate gets an hour off. Book the class ahead, it's small-group and time-slotted.

10

Michter's Fort Nelson

Working Distillery Walk-In Friendly
801 W Main St Tours available + cocktail bar

The most beautiful building on Whiskey Row. Michter's restored the historic Fort Nelson building into a working distillery, running the restored Pennsylvania pot still system from their legacy operation, with one of the better cocktail bars in the city upstairs. You don't need a tour ticket to visit the The Bar at Fort Nelson, walk in for a cocktail and stay as long as you like. The bar program is serious, the space is stunning, and they pour well-aged Michter's products you won't find at the grocery store.

If you want the full distillery tour, book ahead, it's a smaller operation and tour times are limited. But the bar alone is worth the stop, and it's the right place to land in the late afternoon.

The Official Trail Starting Point: KBT Welcome Center

Two doors west of Michter's, inside the Frazier History Museum at 829 W Main St, is the official Kentucky Bourbon Trail Welcome Center. It's the designated starting point for the whole trail, with staff who can talk through distillery logistics across the state, plus exhibits worth an hour if bourbon history interests you. It costs nothing to walk into the Welcome Center itself. Treat it as a natural first stop if you're beginning your entire Kentucky trip in Louisville, or as a last one on this walk since it sits between Michter's and Peerless.

11

Kentucky Peerless Distilling Co.

Working Distillery Book Ahead
120 N 10th St One block north of Main

The west anchor of the Row, and the stop most walking guides leave off because it's one block north of Main rather than on it. That's a mistake. Peerless is fully grain-to-bottle: they mill, mash, ferment, distill, age, and bottle their rye and bourbon in the same building, which makes it one of the most complete production tours in the corridor and a real contrast to the tasting rooms you just walked past.

The family history is the hook, a pre-Prohibition distillery revived by the founder's descendants with the original distilled spirits plant number. Book ahead; it's a working floor and group sizes are limited. If you only have room for two proper tours on Whiskey Row, Peerless and Angel's Envy are the bookends to pick.

The Louisville Slugger Museum & Factory

800 W Main St, right on Whiskey Row. You cannot miss it: there's a 120-foot steel baseball bat leaning against the building. The factory tour walks you through how bats are made, from the wood turning to the branding, and every visitor gets a mini bat souvenir on the way out. It runs about 60–90 minutes.

This is the perfect strategic break between morning tours and afternoon tastings. Your palate gets a reset, your feet get off the pavement, and non-bourbon-drinking travel companions get something that's actually great on its own terms. Ticket prices are reasonable. If you have kids or are traveling with someone who's been politely tolerating bourbon experiences all day, do not skip this.

Worth knowing: Big Bat Bourbon (listed above) runs its custom blending class out of this same building, so the group can split for an hour and reconvene. One half tours the bat factory, the other half blends and bottles a bourbon, and nobody has to pretend to enjoy something they didn't want to do.

Strategic Timing

Book Evan Williams and Old Forester for the morning, get them done before lunch. Do the Louisville Slugger during that mid-afternoon stretch when your palate is tired and another tasting sounds unappealing. Then hit the walk-in spots (Buzzard's Roost, Green River, Michter's bar) in the late afternoon when you just want to sip without a guided tour.

The Speakeasies & New Openings (2025–2026)

Whiskey Row and the surrounding blocks have seen an unusual amount of new openings in the past two years. These are worth knowing about before you go.

WhistlePig "The Vault"

Now Open
403 E Market St Tue–Sat 10am–5pm $50–$300 per person

Vermont-based WhistlePig's first Kentucky location is in a converted 1911 bank building, they kept the original vault, which now houses rare and allocated releases. The bar kept the building's original pneumatic tube system (the kind banks used to shuttle cash between tellers) and actually uses it to mix and deliver drinks, you can watch the tubes in action from your seat, which is the kind of detail that makes a place worth visiting even before the whiskey enters the equation.

Three tasting tiers: a $50 hosted intro tasting (groups of 4–10, 45–60 min), a $250 Vault Collection experience featuring their rarest bottles, and the $300 Vault Experience for up to 6 guests with hands-on barrel selection. All seated experiences require a reservation; the cocktail bar is walk-in friendly. WhistlePig makes rye, not bourbon, which is a good palate change mid-day if you're tiring of wheaters and corn-forward mash bills.

Chicken Cock "The Coupe" at Circa 1856

Relaunched March 2026
821 E Market St, NuLu Cocktail destination

Chicken Cock's NuLu speakeasy experience just relaunched in March 2026, leaning fully into the brand's 1856 heritage. The Coupe is designed as a cocktail destination first, the cocktail program here is more sophisticated than the Lawrenceburg tasting room. If you've been to Chicken Cock's main facility and found the accessible tasting area limited, this is the more polished expression of the brand. Worth a stop on your NuLu walk.

Whiskey Thief Distilling Co.

Walk-In Friendly
NuLu Tasting room + listening lounge

Formerly Three Boys Farm Distillery, now rebranded as Whiskey Thief Distilling Co. The Louisville tasting room and listening lounge offers a barrel thieving experience where you fill your own bottle at barrel strength, which is exactly the kind of tactile souvenir that makes for a good story. They host live jazz, which is either a selling point or a liability depending on when you arrive. A different vibe from the historic Whiskey Row spots, and the better for it.

The Last Refuge

NuLu
NuLu Bob Dylan's whiskey and arts space

Bob Dylan's whiskey venture, Heaven's Door, converted a 150-year-old NuLu church into a combined whiskey experience and arts space. The building alone is worth seeing. The Last Refuge functions as a bar, tasting room, and gallery, rotating art installations alongside pours of Heaven's Door's lineup. It's unusual for a bourbon trail stop, more atmosphere and concept than deep whiskey education, but that's part of the point. Fits well as a mid-afternoon stop before dinner.

NuLu: Walk East from Whiskey Row

The NuLu (New Louisville) district runs along East Market Street, roughly 10 minutes on foot east from the heart of Whiskey Row. The neighborhood has its own cluster of distillery experiences plus the best restaurant options within walking distance of the whole corridor.

NuLu Distilleries

Rabbit Hole (NuLu), modern architecture, stunning building, tours worth booking. Copper & Kings (Butchertown, short rideshare), American brandy operation, completely different from everything else on this list, worth the trip if you want to break out of the bourbon bubble. Angel's Envy sits at the NuLu end of Main Street too, but it opens the walking route above rather than working as an add-on.

Where to Eat & Drink

La Bodeguita de Mima: Top Pick for Dinner

725 E Market St, NuLu. This is the restaurant. It's a 1950s Havana-style Cuban place with a rum bar, cigar lounge, and live music most nights. The Cuban sandwich is as good as it gets outside Miami, the ropa vieja is outstanding, and the mojitos are made properly, muddled lime and fresh mint, not a pre-mix situation.

Make a reservation for dinner. The place gets packed and they don't have a ton of seats. It's about a 15-minute walk from the west end of Whiskey Row or a short rideshare. After a day of bourbon, Cuban food and rum cocktails is the ideal landing point, the food actually tastes like something after your palate has been demolished by high-proof samples all afternoon.

Whiskey Row Lunch & Bar Options

  • Doc Crow's: Whiskey Row, BBQ and a large bourbon selection. Solid lunch stop in the middle of the route. The smoked brisket holds up well as a mid-day meal between tastings.
  • Merle's Whiskey Kitchen: Southern food and live music on Whiskey Row. More of an evening spot, but the food is good if you're there in the afternoon.
  • Sidebar at Whiskey Row: Burgers and bourbon flights. Casual, quick, good for groups who don't want to commit to a full sit-down lunch.
  • Monk's Road Boiler House: The upscale option on Whiskey Row (listed above). If you want a proper meal mid-route rather than bar food, this is the move.

Where to Buy Bottles

Evergreen Liquors

Employee-Owned
720 E Market St, NuLu Tasting bar inside

This is where you buy bottles to take home. Not the distillery gift shops, here. Evergreen is a massive, employee-owned independent liquor store with a tasting bar inside, knowledgeable staff, and store-pick single barrel selections you won't find anywhere else. The prices are fair (better than most gift shops), the selection covers allocated bottles that most stores don't carry, and the staff will actually help you make a decision rather than just upselling the most expensive thing on the shelf.

Hit this on your way to dinner. You'll have had enough tastings by that point to know what you liked during the day, which makes for better buying decisions than shopping at 11am before you've tasted anything.

Bottle Buying Strategy

Distillery gift shops are great for exclusive releases and bottles only available at that specific location. Evergreen is better for everything else, broader selection, better prices, and staff who've tasted most of what they sell. Do both, but prioritize the gift shops for distillery-exclusive bottles and Evergreen for everything you want to drink at home.

Practical Tips

  • Car-free day: This is the one place on the Bourbon Trail where you genuinely don't need a car for the day. Uber and Lyft are abundant in Louisville. Start at your hotel, spend the whole day on foot, take a rideshare to dinner.
  • Wear comfortable shoes. You'll walk 2–3 miles total. The pavement on Main Street is uneven in places, this isn't a flip-flop situation.
  • Cap yourself at 3–4 tasting stops. More than that and your palate stops working. Quality over quantity, four good tastings beats seven mediocre ones.
  • Book in advance: Angel's Envy, Old Forester, Evan Williams, Peerless, Pursuit Spirits, and the Big Bat blending class all want a reservation, and the first three fill up on weekends. Buzzard's Roost, Green River, Bardstown Bourbon Co., and the Michter's bar are walk-in friendly, which makes them the right afternoon flex. Go Tuesday–Thursday if you can.
  • Pacing matters: Morning tours at the big distilleries → Louisville Slugger break → afternoon walk-in tastings at the newer spots → Evergreen for bottles → dinner. This order works.
  • Two days is better than one. If you're staying in Louisville for a weekend, split the Whiskey Row stops across two days. You'll get more out of each experience when you're not rushing.

Nearby Louisville Distilleries (Short Rideshare)

These don't fit on a pure walking route but are close enough to work as an add-on if you have extra time or a car:

  • Rabbit Hole (NuLu), modern architecture, worth booking a tour. A short walk from Evergreen.
  • Copper & Kings (Butchertown), American brandy, completely different from the rest of the strip. Short rideshare from NuLu. Worth it for the novelty alone.
  • Stitzel-Weller (Shively), historic distillery about 10 minutes by car. Go for the grounds and history; the gift shop reliably carries Blade & Bow, and you may get lucky with an Orphan Barrel release, but don't count on it.
