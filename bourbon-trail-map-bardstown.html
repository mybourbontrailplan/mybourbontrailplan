Bardstown Bourbon Trail Map

Bardstown calls itself the Bourbon Capital of the World and the map backs it up: a tight cluster of distilleries inside a five-minute drive of the town square, with the rest radiating out across about fifty minutes of two-lane road.

Densest cluster outside Whiskey Row Parking free almost everywhere Maker's Mark 20 to 25 min south Best base for a multi-day trip

How These Distilleries Cluster

This region rewards a base more than a route. Almost everything is measured from the Bardstown square, and the difference between the groups below is not really distance, it is whether you are on town streets or on rural highway where a wrong turn costs you fifteen minutes.

The town core

All inside about 5 minutes

Heaven Hill and Willett are 0.8 miles apart, Preservation is under two miles from either, and Chicken Cock is on the square itself and walkable from anywhere downtown. This is the densest bourbon cluster outside Whiskey Row, and it is the reason to stay in Bardstown rather than commute in.

The east edge

0.6 miles apart, 5 to 8 minutes from town

Lux Row and Bardstown Bourbon Company sit barely half a mile from each other on the Parkway side of town. Pair them without hesitation. Bardstown Bourbon Co. also has Bottle & Bond on site, which makes this the pairing that solves lunch.

South to Loretto and Lebanon

20 to 25 minutes out, 10 minutes apart

Maker's Mark and Limestone Branch are about six miles apart, so once you have committed to the drive south you should do both. The road out to Loretto is genuinely beautiful and genuinely rural: two lanes, no shoulder in places, and no rideshare coming to collect you.

The separate runs

Log Still 15 min, Jim Beam 20 min, Boundary Oak 25 min

Log Still sits south at New Haven, Jim Beam northwest at Clermont, and Boundary Oak west near Fort Knox. Each is a different direction from town, which is why the widest pair on this map, Limestone Branch to Boundary Oak, is about fifty minutes apart. Pick one direction per day.

A Sensible One-Day Loop

The efficient day. Spend the morning in the town core, where three tours are realistic because the driving between them is measured in minutes: Heaven Hill, then Willett or Preservation. Eat on the square. Then take the afternoon south to Maker's Mark, which is the one that needs unhurried time and the one people most regret rushing.

The arriving-from-Louisville day. Jim Beam at Clermont is on the way in, roughly twenty minutes northwest of Bardstown, so it costs you almost nothing to take it as your first stop rather than a separate trip. Then the town core in the afternoon.

Three distilleries is the honest ceiling here, and a fourth only works if every one of them is in the core. Tours run 60 to 90 minutes, and the rural drives eat the rest.

Where the Lunch Break Falls

The square is the answer for most days. The Old Talbott Tavern is one of the oldest inns in America and the bar downstairs is a destination in its own right; Mammy's Kitchen is the unfussy local option; and Hurst Drug Store is a working pharmacy with a genuine old soda fountain inside, which is a fine ten-minute stop when a full lunch would blow the schedule.

If your day runs through the east edge, Bottle & Bond is on the Bardstown Bourbon Company campus, so lunch happens where you already parked. That is the single best lunch decision in this region, because it removes a drive.

Two things to know. The Rickhouse is dinner only and closed Mondays, so it is not a lunch plan. And it is in Bardstown, not Frankfort, which trips people up. Full detail in our Bardstown eat and drink section.

Parking Realities

This is the easiest parking region on the trail, by a wide margin. Nearly every stop has a free on-site lot, most of them large, and several have room for an RV or a tour van. Heaven Hill and Maker's Mark are effectively never a problem.

The notable exception is Chicken Cock on the square, which has no dedicated lot and relies on street parking along Stephen Foster Avenue and South 3rd, plus a public lot nearby. That is a feature rather than a flaw: it is the one you walk to after parking once downtown for lunch.

Because parking is free and abundant, the real constraint here is not where you leave the car, it is who is driving it. There is no rideshare network on the roads out to Loretto and New Haven. Our transportation guide covers the designated driver arithmetic for exactly this stretch.

Plan the Rest of the Day

Where you sleep changes this region more than in any other. Staying on the square puts Chicken Cock on foot and most of the core inside ten minutes; staying out toward New Hope trades that density for a shorter run at Maker's Mark in the morning. Our lodging guide lists the drive time from each property to each distillery so you can make that trade deliberately.

Neighbouring regions: Louisville is about 50 minutes north and Frankfort about 50 minutes northeast. Both are a separate day, not an add-on.
