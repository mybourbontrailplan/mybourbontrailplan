Frankfort Bourbon Trail Map

The two distilleries furthest apart here are twenty minutes apart. Frankfort is the one region where visiting everything on the map in a single day is not ambitious, it is just the plan.

How These Distilleries Cluster

There is really only one cluster here, which is what makes this region unusual. Buffalo Trace and J. Mattingly 1845 are half a mile apart. Glenns Creek is two miles from either. Castle & Key is about ten minutes out, and the Whiskey Thief farm about fifteen. Nothing is far from anything.

The Buffalo Trace block

Half a mile apart

Buffalo Trace and J. Mattingly 1845 are close enough that the drive between them is not worth calling a drive. Worth knowing: Buffalo Trace is not an official Kentucky Bourbon Trail member. Sazerac left the KDA in 2009 and never rejoined, so it will not appear on official trail materials despite being the most sought-after tour in the state.

Glenns Creek and the Castle

5 and 10 minutes out

Glenns Creek operates on the historic Old Crow site, two miles from Buffalo Trace. Castle & Key is about ten minutes further and is the one to give real time to: the restored buildings and grounds are the draw as much as the whiskey, and the cocktail bar is a legitimate place to spend an hour.

The working farm

About 15 minutes west

Whiskey Thief is a 127-acre working farm distillery where you thieve and bottle your own pour. Food truck and live music Thursday through Sunday, which makes it a better late-afternoon stop than a morning one.

A Sensible One-Day Loop

Book Buffalo Trace first and build the day around it, because the rest of the region will accommodate whatever slot you get. That is a luxury you do not have in Bardstown or Lexington.

New Buffalo Trace dates are released weekly on Wednesdays at 10:00 AM Eastern, each release covering a seven-day window about eight weeks out. Tours are free, which is exactly why they are the hardest reservation in Kentucky. Our booking guide covers the strategy.

A clean loop: Buffalo Trace in your booked slot, walk-or-hop to J. Mattingly either side of it, lunch downtown, then Castle & Key for the afternoon and its grounds. If you have appetite for one more, Glenns Creek slots in almost anywhere because it is two miles from the middle of everything.

Where the Lunch Break Falls

Frankfort is a small state capital, not a dining destination, and it is better to know that going in. Bourbon on Main is the verified bourbon-forward lunch option downtown and the one we use in our 3-day itinerary. Serafini is the sit-down Italian option for dinner.

The alternative worth considering is skipping a restaurant altogether and taking a late lunch at the Castle & Key cocktail bar, which lets the afternoon stop absorb the meal. Similarly, the Whiskey Thief farm runs a food truck Thursday through Sunday.

Two corrections that catch people out. Rick's White Light Diner appears to have closed as of late 2025, so do not build a plan around it. And The Rickhouse is in Bardstown, not Frankfort, despite how often it turns up in Frankfort search results. Our Frankfort eat and drink section has the short honest list.

Parking Realities

Every stop here has free on-site parking, which combined with the short distances makes this the least stressful region to drive.

One caveat that matters. Buffalo Trace's lot has plenty of space on weekdays but genuinely fills on weekends, so arrive 15 to 20 minutes before your tour rather than on the hour. Missing a free tour that took eight weeks to book because you were circling a car park is a bad way to spend a Saturday.

Everything else, Castle & Key, Glenns Creek, J. Mattingly and the Whiskey Thief farm, has free parking on site with no real competition for it.

Plan the Rest of the Day

Frankfort works as a day trip from a base elsewhere, and plenty of people do it that way. It is about 50 minutes from Bardstown and about 30 minutes from Lexington. But staying overnight buys you the first Buffalo Trace slot of the morning and saves an hour of driving after a day of tastings, which is the argument for it.

The other reason to stay: the Lexington and Lawrenceburg region is only about half an hour east, and Woodford Reserve is roughly fifteen minutes from Castle & Key, which means a Frankfort base can reach two regions without a long transfer.
